# Japan Phrase Trainer

An interactive **8-week course for people travelling to Japan**: small static web app (single `index.html`, no build step, no dependencies, no third-party requests: the fonts are self-hosted in `fonts/`). The course is the main focus of the site. It is generic: no dates, no assumed textbook, any departure. Owner/author: Daniel, who is also the first learner. Phrases and UI: English/Japanese. Personal context for sessions on Daniel's machine lives in `CLAUDE.local.md`, which is not tracked.

## Current state
- Course (`WEEKS` in `index.html`): 8 weeks, each with `title`, `summary`, `goals`, `kanji` (signs to recognise, about 32 in all, recognition only), `scenario` {title, text}, optional `grammar` and `tip`, and either `phrases` (practice cards) or `preview` (a first look at the key phrases, shown until the cards exist). Weeks: 1 Meeting people, 2 Numbers/money/first signs, 3 Shopping, 4 Food and restaurants, 5 Getting around, 6 Staying somewhere (+ light ます-form), 7 Sights and temples, 8 Arrival and putting it together. Derived from Daniel's 9-week personal plan by merging consolidation and arrival, and dropping dates and Genki references.
  - Weeks 1 and 2 are built. Weeks 3 to 8 are outlines. The full build-ready content for them (phrases in `WEEK1_PHRASES` format, understand-only items, dialogues, audio budget, native-speaker check list) is in `content-plan.md`, written by Opus on 2026-11-03 and untracked. To promote a week: add `phrases` (drop `preview`), plus `review`/`hear`/`drill`/`scenario.turns` as the plan says, and `test.js` checks the new data. There is no audio step any more (see Speech output).
  - **Week 2 is the template for the rest.** Its deck is 3 `review` cards (`[week, index]` into an earlier week's `phrases`, never a template) followed by 13 own cards; `deckSize(w)` is the count and done-state is by deck position. Besides the card deck a week can have, shown as tabs when there is more than one (`activitiesFor`): **Listening** (`hear`: understand-only items with `note`, optional `reply:{ok,bad}` as indices into the week's own `phrases`; a two-step "what did they say / what would you answer" quiz plus a "What you will hear" list), **Numbers** (`drill:"price"`: type the price you hear), **Signs** (the week's `kanji` as a multiple-choice deck; distractors come from every sign unlocked so far; a round with no slips is "clean", two clean rounds is done), **Scenario** (`scenario.turns`: a scripted dialogue, clerk lines are `hear` items, your turns are options with `ok`/`why`, a turn with `price` first asks you to type the number you just heard). Done-state for signs and scenario is `jpt-week<N>-acts`. A week with a single activity (week 1) shows no tab bar.
  - Prices are generated, never written out: `numberReading(n)` from the `NUM` tables (the irregular 300/600/800/3000/8000 live there once), `PRICES` is 42 integers, `priceItem(n)` makes the phrase, `parseYen` reads what the learner typed (`¥1,280`, full-width digits), `priceHint` points at the wrong unit and never contains the answer (tested). `allPhrases()` includes the price items; `hearPhrases()` are the understand-only items.
  - Playback: everything spoken goes through `playClips`/`stopClips` (a sequence counter cancels older chains). The two voice selects (card and drill) are one setting.
  - Routing is the hash: `#` is the overview, `#week-N` a week; unknown hashes fall back to the overview. `route()` stops audio and the mic on every change. Done-state is per week in localStorage (`jpt-week<N>-done`; week 1 keeps its original key). `[hidden]` is `display:none!important` globally because `.card{display:grid}` would otherwise beat it.
  - `allPhrases()` flattens the weeks; `test.js` uses it.
- The learner's own details (week 1): `#setup` is an onboarding form for first name, country and job, saved in localStorage (`jpt-profile`, never sent anywhere). Week 1 needs it: opening `#week-1` without a profile redirects (with `location.replace`, so Back does not loop) to `#setup` and returns afterwards; "Use an example instead" saves `{example:true}` (Alex, USA, company employee) so no one is stuck. The overview has a "Your details" card, week 1 shows a "Your introduction" block.
  - Phrases with a `slot` (`name`, `country`, `job`) are templates: `{country.k}`, `{name.r}`, `{job.kj}` ... are filled from the profile by `fillPhrase`; `weekPhrases(w, vals)` expands a week; `resolveProfile` turns what is saved into the values (null if it no longer resolves). `COUNTRIES` (43) and `JOBS` (18) are finite lists; each has `k` kana, `r` romaji to type (capital first letter = katakana, so `convert(r) === k` is a test), optional `kj` kanji spelling, countries a `nat` (English nationality), jobs an `e` with its article ("a teacher").
  - Names are typed as romaji for how they sound (DANIERU), never auto-transliterated; `nameToKana` always gives katakana and the form rejects leftover latin letters. Countries and jobs are finite lists; a hand-typed "Other" is not. All of them are read by the device voice like everything else.
  - Done-state is by card position, so `saveProfile` clears the done marks of personal cards when the details change (fixed cards keep theirs, re-saving the same details clears nothing).
  - The form's examples are teaching content: Peru is `PERU-` (ペルー) and a vet is `juui` (じゅうい). Check them when editing.
- Phrase cards (`phrases` of a week): kana, romaji, English, accepted kanji spellings (`alt`), tolerated particle spelling (`soft`, e.g. わ for は).
- Romaji -> kana converter (`convert`): capital first letter = katakana word, `ha` types は, `-` = ー.
- Answer check (`checkAnswer`): normalises hiragana/katakana, accepts kanji variants, per-character diff against the model phrase.
- **Speech output is the device's own Japanese voice. The site ships no audio files.** Decided 2026-11-03: Apple's macOS licence (Software License Agreement §2F, "Voices") allows the System Voices only for "personal, non-commercial use" and expressly bars "publishing or redistribution … in a profit, non-profit, public sharing or commercial context". Rendering Kyoko/Otoya/O-Ren with `say` and putting the files in a public repo or on GitHub Pages would break that, so the earlier pre-rendered `audio/` and `make-audio.js` were removed from the repo and purged from git history. Using the voices live on each visitor's own device is fine.
  - Voices: `japaneseVoices`/`voiceQuality`/`rankVoices`/`voiceStatus` are pure (top of the script, tested in `test.js`). `voiceTier` reads the quality from the name and from `voiceURI`, because iOS names the compact and the enhanced voice identically ("Kyoko" twice, reported 2026-11-04) and the identifier should be the only difference (`com.apple.voice.enhanced...` vs `...compact...`; assumed from macOS, **not yet seen on a real iPhone**, and some browsers set `voiceURI` to the name). `voiceOptions` makes picker labels ("Kyoko (Enhanced)", "Kyoko (Basic)") and keys unique even when nothing tells voices apart; the help block prints the current voice's technical name so a learner can report it. There are also old reports of iOS honouring only one voice per locale, so check that the picker really changes the sound. `rankVoices` ranks Premium > Enhanced or Natural (Edge) > a named standard voice > anything else, and never lists Apple's novelty voices (Eddy, Flo, Grandma, Grandpa, Reed, Rocko, Sandy, Shelley), which are all tagged ja-JP and sort ahead of Kyoko. Never just take the first `ja-JP` match.
  - UI: the "Voice" select (card and price drill, one setting, remembered in localStorage as the option key under `jpt-voice`) lists the device's Japanese voices. A `.voice-slot` under the buttons shows which voice speaks and a `good`/`basic`/`none` status line, plus a "Sounds off? Improve the voice" help block (iPhone/iPad, Mac, Android, Windows) cloned from `#voiceHelpT`. `renderVoices` re-runs on `voiceschanged` because `getVoices()` is empty until the list loads. If you add a place that speaks, add a `.voice-slot` there.
  - Slow = one utterance per chunk (the spaces in `s`) with a pause between (`SLOW_GAP`), at a near-normal rate (`SLOW_RATE` 0.9). A stretched utterance smears Apple's compact voices. Multi-item sequences (dialogues) are chained with `ITEM_GAP`.
  - `s` on a phrase is still the spelling given to the engine (kanji where the reading is unambiguous, so は is read as a particle and words segment properly). **Not verified** with a real device voice: that those kanji spellings are read as the kana say. Listen to 人 as じん, 医者, 看護師 and the like before the site goes public.
  - On macOS 27 the voices live under System Settings → Accessibility → **Read & Speak** ("Spoken Content" was renamed). Japanese seems to have no Premium tier, so Enhanced is the ceiling for Apple voices.
  - Tested in the in-app browser with the real macOS voice list and with `getVoices`/`speak` stubbed (good, basic, none, picker persistence, chained slow take, interruption). **Not tested** on a real iPhone/iPad, whose Safari should expose downloaded Enhanced voices but is unverified; try it before relying on it.
  - If recordings are ever wanted again: Google Cloud TTS (ja-JP Neural2/WaveNet; Chirp 3 HD) looked viable (its docs say the audio may be used to power applications; free tier 1M characters/month is far more than this course needs; billing must be enabled), but the full Cloud ToS were not read, the voices were not listened to, and the Generative-AI terms bar services aimed at under-18s (unclear whether plain TTS counts). It returns MP3/WAV, not m4a. The old `make-audio.js` is in git history (`git log --diff-filter=D -- make-audio.js`).
- Two card toggles: "Hide kana, recall from English" (session only) and "Show romaji" (off by default, remembered in localStorage). Romaji is also suppressed while kana is hidden, so it cannot give the answer away.
- Speech input: a "Speak" button (shown only when `SpeechRecognition`/`webkitSpeechRecognition` exists), lang `ja-JP`, `maxAlternatives` 5, interim results shown as "Hearing: ...". The final transcript goes into the answer field and through `checkAnswer`, so `alt`, `soft` and the diff apply to speech exactly as to typing. `bestAlternative(p, alts)` (pure, in `test.js`) takes the first candidate that passes, else the top one.
  - **Interim-only results count.** Found in live testing 2026-11-03: a browser can show "Hearing: ..." and then end without ever flagging `isFinal`, and the text used to be dropped ("Did not catch anything", empty answer). `startMic` keeps the last interim alternatives and finishes with them when the recognizer ends, errors, or has given no new result for `QUIET_MS` (1.5 s, then `stop()`).
  - **Dictation worked only once on iPhone** (reported 2026-11-04). Other developers report iOS Safari delivering a result and then going silent, with no `end` or `error` and the mic still on. A recognizer is therefore `abort()`ed as soon as it has answered, and no state waits for `end`: `stop()` has a watchdog (`STOP_WAIT_MS`), listening has a limit (`LISTEN_MS`), and events from an old recognizer are ignored (`rec !== r`). A scripted recognizer that blocks new ones until the old is released reproduced the symptom on the old code and passes on the new. **Not yet confirmed on a real iPhone**, which is the only real test: neither Xcode nor the simulator is available on this Mac.
  - The UI resets on the result, not on `end`: browsers are unreliable about `end`, and a stuck "Stop" button would be bad. `end` still handles "heard nothing". Navigation aborts the recognizer (`stopMic`).
  - Playback and speech synthesis are stopped when the mic starts (the recognizer must not hear the page, and iOS cannot do both).
  - Errors map to plain messages in `MIC_ERRORS` (`not-allowed`, `service-not-allowed` = Dictation off on iOS, `no-speech`, `network`, `audio-capture`, `language-not-supported`); unknown codes show the raw code.
  - Tested only with a scripted fake recognizer injected into an iframe (all flows and errors), never with a real voice. Real accuracy on a Japanese voice, iOS Safari and the permission prompts are unverified until tried on the iPad/iPhone. Needs https or localhost; will not work from `file://` in Safari.
- Done-state in localStorage.
- Pure logic is at the top of the script and testable in Node: `node test.js`.

## Why this moved out of the Claude artifact
The artifact sandbox blocks the microphone. As a normal https site (GitHub Pages) the page can use the Web Speech API (`webkitSpeechRecognition`, lang `ja-JP`) directly. That is the main next step.

## Conventions
- Keep it dependency-free and mobile-first (iPad/iPhone Safari). Inputs >= 16px, touch targets >= 44px.
- Light/dark via CSS tokens in `:root`, no hard-coded colours elsewhere.
- Verify any change to converter/check logic with `node test.js` before committing.
- After adding or editing phrases, run `node test.js`; there is nothing to render. `index.html` is about 1,800 lines now; moving `WEEKS` and the lists into their own file(s) is the obvious next tidy-up once weeks 3 to 8 land.
- Git: `main`, remote `origin` = https://github.com/Agentkramer/japanese-phrase-trainer (public; GitHub's initial commit holds the MIT `LICENSE`, our history sits on top of it). Commits use the GitHub noreply address (`135695431+Agentkramer@users.noreply.github.com`, set in this repo's git config); never put Daniel's private address in history. `.gitignore` excludes `audio/` (a leftover local folder of the old Apple-voice renders, unused, must never be committed), `japan-schedule.md` (private plans), `CLAUDE.local.md` (personal context for Claude sessions), `content-plan.md` and `.claude/settings.local.json`. `content-plan.md` (Opus's build-ready plan for weeks 3 to 8) is ignored too, so it exists only on Daniel's machine; if you work from a fresh clone it is not there.
- Fonts: `fonts/fonts.css` self-hosts Noto Sans JP (one variable file per slice, weights 400-700) and Shippori Mincho (500 and 700), both SIL OFL 1.1 (licence texts in `fonts/`). They were self-hosted because loading from Google sends every visitor's IP address to Google, which a German court ruled unlawful in 2022; the page makes no third-party requests, keep it that way. Only the unicode-range slices that contain characters used in `index.html` were downloaded (93 files, 1.5 MB, the browser fetches only the slices a page needs); other characters fall back to the system Japanese font. **If you add a character that is not already on the site (a new kanji, a country name in a new script), check it renders in the web font; if it falls back, re-download the matching slices** from the Google Fonts CSS (`fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+JP:wght@400;500;700&family=Shippori+Mincho:wght@500;700`, fetched with a Chrome user agent to get woff2 slices) and add them to `fonts/fonts.css`.
- `.claude/launch.json` serves the folder on :8777 for previewing. Speech recognition needs http(s) or localhost.
