# Japan Phrase Trainer

An interactive **8-week course for people travelling to Japan**: small static web app (single `index.html`, no build step, no dependencies except Google Fonts). The course is the main focus of the site. It is generic: no dates, no assumed textbook, any departure. Owner/author: Daniel, German teacher/Informatik, who is also the first learner. Chat and explanations with him: English. Phrases and UI: English/Japanese. His own dated plan lives in `japan-schedule.md`, which is deliberately untracked and not part of the site.

## Current state
- Course (`WEEKS` in `index.html`): 8 weeks, each with `title`, `summary`, `goals`, `kanji` (signs to recognise, about 32 in all, recognition only), `scenario` {title, text}, optional `grammar` and `tip`, and either `phrases` (practice cards) or `preview` (a first look at the key phrases, shown until the cards exist). Weeks: 1 Meeting people, 2 Numbers/money/first signs, 3 Shopping, 4 Food and restaurants, 5 Getting around, 6 Staying somewhere (+ light ます-form), 7 Sights and temples, 8 Arrival and putting it together. Derived from Daniel's 9-week personal plan by merging consolidation and arrival, and dropping dates and Genki references.
  - Weeks 1 and 2 are built. Weeks 3 to 8 are outlines. The full build-ready content for them (phrases in `WEEK1_PHRASES` format, understand-only items, dialogues, audio budget, native-speaker check list) is in `content-plan.md`, written by Opus on 2026-11-03 and untracked. To promote a week: add `phrases` (drop `preview`), plus `review`/`hear`/`drill`/`scenario.turns` as the plan says, run `node make-audio.js`, and `test.js` checks the new data.
  - **Week 2 is the template for the rest.** Its deck is 3 `review` cards (`[week, index]` into an earlier week's `phrases`, never a template) followed by 13 own cards; `deckSize(w)` is the count and done-state is by deck position. Besides the card deck a week can have, shown as tabs when there is more than one (`activitiesFor`): **Listening** (`hear`: understand-only items with `note`, one recording `v`, normal speed only, optional `reply:{ok,bad}` as indices into the week's own `phrases`; a two-step "what did they say / what would you answer" quiz plus a "What you will hear" list), **Numbers** (`drill:"price"`: type the price you hear), **Signs** (the week's `kanji` as a multiple-choice deck; distractors come from every sign unlocked so far; a round with no slips is "clean", two clean rounds is done), **Scenario** (`scenario.turns`: a scripted dialogue, clerk lines are `hear` items, your turns are options with `ok`/`why`, a turn with `price` first asks you to type the number you just heard). Done-state for signs and scenario is `jpt-week<N>-acts`. A week with a single activity (week 1) shows no tab bar.
  - Prices are generated, never written out: `numberReading(n)` from the `NUM` tables (the irregular 300/600/800/3000/8000 live there once), `PRICES` is 42 integers, `priceItem(n)` makes the phrase, `parseYen` reads what the learner typed (`¥1,280`, full-width digits), `priceHint` points at the wrong unit and never contains the answer (tested). `allPhrases()` includes the price items so they get 3 voices, normal and slow; `hearPhrases()` are rendered separately.
  - Playback: all clips go through `playClips`/`stopClips` (a sequence counter cancels older chains; `AbortError` is ignored). The two voice selects (card and drill) are one setting.
  - Routing is the hash: `#` is the overview, `#week-N` a week; unknown hashes fall back to the overview. `route()` stops audio and the mic on every change. Done-state is per week in localStorage (`jpt-week<N>-done`; week 1 keeps its original key). `[hidden]` is `display:none!important` globally because `.card{display:grid}` would otherwise beat it.
  - `allPhrases()` flattens the weeks; `make-audio.js` and `test.js` use it. Audio files are flat per voice (`audio/<voice>/<id>.m4a`), so two weeks sharing a phrase share its file; `test.js` fails if two different phrases get the same audio id.
- The learner's own details (week 1): `#setup` is an onboarding form for first name, country and job, saved in localStorage (`jpt-profile`, never sent anywhere). Week 1 needs it: opening `#week-1` without a profile redirects (with `location.replace`, so Back does not loop) to `#setup` and returns afterwards; "Use an example instead" saves `{example:true}` (Alex, USA, company employee) so no one is stuck. The overview has a "Your details" card, week 1 shows a "Your introduction" block.
  - Phrases with a `slot` (`name`, `country`, `job`) are templates: `{country.k}`, `{name.r}`, `{job.kj}` ... are filled from the profile by `fillPhrase`; `weekPhrases(w, vals)` expands a week; `resolveProfile` turns what is saved into the values (null if it no longer resolves). `COUNTRIES` (43) and `JOBS` (18) are finite lists; each has `k` kana, `r` romaji to type (capital first letter = katakana, so `convert(r) === k` is a test), optional `kj` kanji spelling, countries a `nat` (English nationality), jobs an `e` with its article ("a teacher").
  - Audio: every country and job is pre-rendered (`allPhrases()` expands them, so `make-audio.js` and `test.js` see them). A name cannot be, and neither can a hand-typed "Other" country or job. Those cards are `live`: `speak()` skips the file and uses the device voice, and the page says why. Names are typed as romaji for how they sound (DANIERU), never auto-transliterated; `nameToKana` always gives katakana and the form rejects leftover latin letters.
  - Done-state is by card position, so `saveProfile` clears the done marks of personal cards when the details change (fixed cards keep theirs, re-saving the same details clears nothing).
  - The form's examples are teaching content: Peru is `PERU-` (ペルー) and a vet is `juui` (じゅうい). Check them when editing.
- Phrase cards (`phrases` of a week): kana, romaji, English, accepted kanji spellings (`alt`), tolerated particle spelling (`soft`, e.g. わ for は).
- Romaji -> kana converter (`convert`): capital first letter = katakana word, `ha` types は, `-` = ー.
- Answer check (`checkAnswer`): normalises hiragana/katakana, accepts kanji variants, per-character diff against the model phrase.
- Audio: pre-rendered files in `audio/<voice>/` (`<id>.m4a`, `<id>.slow.m4a`, id from `audioId()` = slugged romaji), generated by `node make-audio.js`. Three voices (`VOICES` in `index.html`: kyoko, otoya, oren), chosen with a "Voice" select on the card and remembered in localStorage (`jpt-voice`). The page plays the file and falls back to `speechSynthesis` only when there is none.
  - Fall back only on a real failure: an interrupted `play()` (a newer click) rejects with `AbortError`, which must not trigger the fallback or the device voice speaks over the recording.
  - `s` on a phrase is the spelling given to the speech engine (kanji where the reading is unambiguous, so は is read as a particle and words segment properly); spaces in `s` are the pause points of the slow take.
  - Slow = a pause between chunks at near-normal rate, not a stretched utterance, which smears Apple's compact voices.
  - Voice selection in the fallback must never take the first `ja-JP` match: Apple's novelty voices (Eddy, Flo, Grandma, Grandpa, Reed, Rocko, Sandy, Shelley) are all tagged ja-JP and sort ahead of Kyoko.
  - `make-audio.js` renders every voice in `VOICES` (`--voice <id>` for just one, e.g. `--voice otoya`). **Files that already exist are kept**, so a run only renders what is new and git sees no churn; re-rendering a file that is already there gives different bytes with identical decoded audio. Pass `--force` after changing a voice or an existing phrase's `s`/`k`. `hear` items are rendered once, in the voice they name, normal speed only. Per voice it takes the first identifier in `say` that actually renders. `say -v <id>` accepts an identifier for a voice that is only registered, and renders silence rather than failing, so every candidate and every take is checked by duration. A voice with nothing installed is skipped with a warning and its old files stay.
  - Always use full identifiers, never display names: bare `say -v Kyoko` resolves to the Enhanced voice, and the list shows two entries named Kyoko. O-Ren is a Siri voice, so her identifiers are `com.apple.ttsbundle.siri_O-ren_ja-JP_premium` (shown as "O-Ren (Enhanced)") and `..._compact`, not the `com.apple.voice.*` pattern of Kyoko and Otoya.
  - Verified mechanically (render the sentence with は and again with わ, compare decoded audio; a control confirmed は inside a word differs): all three voices read a particle は as "wa" in every week 1 and week 2 sentence, kana-only ones included. **Not verified:** that the kanji speech spellings (`s`) are read as the kana say. Comparing the kanji and kana renderings byte for byte is useless for this, because the engine chooses different accents for them even when the sounds agree (64 of 80 differed). Listen to 人 as じん, 医者, 看護師 and the like before the site goes public.
  - As of 2026-11-03 everything is rendered with the Enhanced voices. Premium Kyoko stays a stub even with everything downloaded; Japanese appears to have no Premium tier, so Enhanced is the ceiling for Apple voices here. On macOS 27 the voices live under System Settings → Accessibility → **Read & Speak** ("Spoken Content" was renamed; the internal id is still SpokenContent).
- Two card toggles: "Hide kana, recall from English" (session only) and "Show romaji" (off by default, remembered in localStorage). Romaji is also suppressed while kana is hidden, so it cannot give the answer away.
- Speech input: a "Speak" button (shown only when `SpeechRecognition`/`webkitSpeechRecognition` exists), lang `ja-JP`, `maxAlternatives` 5, interim results shown as "Hearing: ...". The final transcript goes into the answer field and through `checkAnswer`, so `alt`, `soft` and the diff apply to speech exactly as to typing. `bestAlternative(p, alts)` (pure, in `test.js`) takes the first candidate that passes, else the top one.
  - The UI resets on the final result, not on `end`: browsers are unreliable about `end`, and a stuck "Stop" button after a correct answer would be bad. `end` still handles "heard nothing". A newer start/navigation aborts the old recognizer; its late events are ignored via `rec !== r`.
  - Playback and speech synthesis are stopped when the mic starts (the recognizer must not hear the page, and iOS cannot do both).
  - Errors map to plain messages in `MIC_ERRORS` (`not-allowed`, `service-not-allowed` = Dictation off on iOS, `no-speech`, `network`, `audio-capture`, `language-not-supported`); unknown codes show the raw code.
  - Tested only with a scripted fake recognizer injected into an iframe (all flows and errors), never with a real voice. Real accuracy on a Japanese voice, iOS Safari and the permission prompts are unverified until tried on the iPad/iPhone. Needs https or localhost; will not work from `file://` in Safari.
- Done-state in localStorage.
- Pure logic is at the top of the script and testable in Node: `node test.js`.

## Why this moved out of the Claude artifact
The artifact sandbox blocks the microphone. As a normal https site (GitHub Pages) the page can use the Web Speech API (`webkitSpeechRecognition`, lang `ja-JP`) directly. That is the main next step.

## Conventions
- Keep it dependency-free and mobile-first (iPad/iPhone Safari). Inputs >= 16px, touch targets >= 44px.
- Light/dark via CSS tokens in `:root`, no hard-coded colours elsewhere.
- Verify any change to converter/check logic with `node test.js` before committing.
- After adding or editing phrases, re-run `node make-audio.js` (`--force` if you changed an existing phrase). It reads the phrases and `VOICES` straight out of `index.html` (same `vm` trick as `test.js`), so those lists stay the single source of truth. `index.html` is about 1,750 lines now; moving `WEEKS` and the lists into their own file(s) is the obvious next tidy-up once weeks 3 to 8 land.
- Git: `main`, local only so far. Commit audio together with the phrase or voice change that produced it. `.claude/settings.local.json` is ignored.
- `.claude/launch.json` serves the folder on :8777 for previewing; the audio only resolves over http, not from a `file://` page.
