An 8-week course for your first trip

Japan Phrase Trainer

Learn to greet people, shop, eat, get around and ask for help, and to read the signs you will actually see. About 20–30 minutes a day, plus one scenario a week. There are no dates: start when your trip is eight weeks away.

    How the course works
    • Set phrases first. Grammar only where it helps on the road. The aim is to say what you need and to recognise what you hear.
    • Kana comes first. You should be able to read hiragana and katakana, slowly is fine. If not, spend a few days on them before week 1, then keep them fresh with a short drill now and then.
    • Signs are recognition only. About 30 kanji you will see in stations, shops and temples. You do not need to write them.
    • One scenario a week. Each week ends with a situation to act out: a convenience store, a restaurant, a station, a hotel. Say it out loud.
    • Safety net. Before you leave, download the offline Japanese pack in Google Translate and try Google Lens on signs and menus.
    • Typing and dictation. Type romaji and press Return to keep hiragana, rather than picking kanji suggestions. Dictation works best as a short pronunciation check, not as the main way to converse.

      How to type and dictate
      • Type romaji and kana appears. Add a space between words.
      • A capital first letter makes a word katakana: DOITSU becomes ドイツ. Use “-” for the long mark ー.
      • The particle は is typed ha, even though it is read “wa”.
      • Tap Speak and say the phrase. What the browser heard appears in the answer field and is checked straight away. Recognition often returns kanji. These phrases accept the usual kanji spellings, so a miss means it heard something different. Say it again, a little slower.
      • Speak uses your browser’s speech service, so it needs an internet connection and permission to use the microphone. If there is no Speak button, this browser has no speech recognition. Tap the microphone on the keyboard while a Japanese keyboard is active instead.
      • Listen is read aloud by your device’s own Japanese voice, so it sounds different on different devices and is better with an Enhanced voice installed (see “Sounds off?” under the buttons). “Listen slowly” is the same reading with a pause between the parts, not a stretched one.

        Listen, then type the price as digits. Listen slowly pauses between the parts. Use the table below if you are not sure how a number is built.

        How Japanese numbers are built

        Each unit is a digit plus a unit word: 380 is three hundred, eight tens: さんびゃく はちじゅう. Highlighted readings are the irregular ones. 100 is ひゃく and 1,000 is せん with no いち, but 10,000 is いちまん.
